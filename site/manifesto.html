Farmologic/Manifesto

Farmologic

Nothing to hide

The things that hold true across every product we put our name to.

Ever noticed that the healthier something claims to be, the less you actually learn from it?

We think we know why, and it starts a long way from any shop shelf. Farmologic was founded by Mandeep Mahadevaiah, who comes from a family that has farmed for generations.

That's not a line for a label. Growing up with the land teaches you cultivation and patience, and the worth of producing something by care rather than by shortcut. It also teaches you exactly where the lying starts.

Where it starts

It doesn't start on the farm.

Ask anyone who grows for a living. What leaves your field and what arrives in a kitchen are two different things. In between, a dozen hands each take a small, forgivable cut of the truth. Nobody in that chain thinks of themselves as a criminal. Everyone is just doing what the market does.

By the time it reaches you, there's nothing left to dilute.

We don't call that lying in this country. We call it market practice.

And we couldn't do a thing about it.

That's the part nobody warns you about, being the honest one. You can't follow your own crop. You can't audit the man who bought it from the man who bought it from you. You do everything right, and then it leaves your hands and becomes somebody else's arithmetic.

So being honest was worth nothing by the time it reached a stranger. That family's decency did not survive the journey to your kitchen. Good intentions aren't a measurement. Care isn't a number.

The problem

Why wellness is worse

And food is the easy case.

With food, somebody somewhere can catch it. Milk you might taste. Oil you might smell. Grain you might see. There are inspectors, surveys, headlines, a furious aunt.

With wellness there is none of that.

You cannot taste potency. You cannot see purity. You will never find out whether it worked, because you never knew what it was in the first place. The declaration on the box could say anything, and your body will not file a complaint.

That's what makes wellness the perfect crime. Not because the people in it are worse. Because it is the one aisle where nobody ever has to be careful.

And the strangest part is that the law already tries. Under India's rules for health supplements and nutraceuticals, every pack must list its ingredients in order and declare how much of each active ingredient it contains. It's printed. It's on the box in your hand. And you still know nothing.

Because a number nobody ever checks is just a story with digits in it. The problem was never secrecy. Everyone declares. The problem is that between the declaring and the believing, there is nothing at all.

And the only answer to a thing you can't see is a number: measured, printed, and put somewhere you can hold it against us.

What

What this is

Underneath whatever Farmologic makes, there's a list of things we've decided not to do.

Our founder's own path led a long way from the farm first: an MS in Psychology in the United States, an MA in Political Science, years spent across disciplines with no obvious connection to soil. They kept returning him to the same three things, though: nature, science, and building something with purpose. Farmologic is where those three finally met.

The idea was to treat a natural ingredient as something to be understood, cultivated, tested and developed with purpose, rather than simply marketed. And to commit to that plainly, in writing, before it is convenient: a way of working we won't bend, and a list anyone can hold us to.

Most brands publish their standard once they're already winning. By then it isn't a standard, it's a description: a flattering account of what they were going to do anyway.

So ours goes first.

Every line below is something you can hold up against what we actually do, later, when it would suit us a great deal better to have said nothing. A standard written down in advance is a stick you can beat us with. That's what it's for.

Where

What you can hold us to

Where this goes, and where it stops. Eight commitments, sorted into the four places it's easiest to cut a corner. Each one holds across every product Farmologic makes.

On the label

what's printed

  1. 01

    Every claim carries the number that backs it.

    If it can't be measured, it doesn't go on the pack.

  2. 02

    The number that could hurt us is printed too.

    The one measure that would expose a shortcut, set right beside the flattering ones.

  3. 03

    What it can't do is written beside what it can.

    In plain words, on the pack, not buried.

In the bottle, and on the bill

what's inside, what it costs

  1. 04

    The whole ingredient, never the cheap thing it was grown on or cut with.

    No filler wearing the ingredient's name.

  2. 05

    The price comes with its reasons.

    What it costs to make, and why the cheap version is cheap.

In the story

what we say about it

  1. 06

    No borrowed mythology.

    No mountains we've never stood on, no monks, no five-thousand-year-old secret we happen to have bottled.

  2. 07

    No cure, no hack, no boost by a multiple.

    Nothing we make is a miracle. Anyone promising more has already started lying.

When it breaks

the one that costs

08

If something goes wrong, you hear it from us first.

Not after it's been noticed. Before.

That last one is the expensive promise. It's also the only one that makes the other seven worth anything.

And a ninth, which isn't a promise so much as a consequence: we will not be the cheapest, ever. Making the real thing costs more than making something that looks like it. That gap is where a great deal of this industry's profit lives. We've decided not to take it.
